10 Critical Thinking Exercises

Critical thinking is a habit, not a talent. These ten drills take about five minutes each and work alone, in class or in a team meeting.

1. Steelman, don't straw-man

Write the strongest version of a view you disagree with, strong enough that its supporters would sign off on it.

2. The 5 Whys

Take a problem and ask "why?" five times to get from symptoms to the root cause.

3. Pre-mortem

Imagine your plan failed a year from now. List the three most likely reasons, then fix them today.

4. Headline audit

Take a news headline. What is the actual claim? What evidence would prove it? What's missing?

5. Base-rate check

Before judging "how likely", ask "how common is this in general?" Most prediction errors start by ignoring base rates.

6. Reverse the question

Instead of "How do we get more users?", ask "What would guarantee we lose users?" Then avoid those things.

7. Fermi estimate

Estimate something unknowable (how many piano tuners work in your city?) by breaking it into guessable parts.

8. Assumption hunt

List every assumption behind a decision. Mark each one as tested or untested.

9. Daily logic puzzle

One structured puzzle a day builds working memory and systematic reasoning. Pick one here.

10. Explain it to a 10-year-old

If you can't explain it simply, you don't understand it yet. Find the gap and fill it.
