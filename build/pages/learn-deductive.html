Deductive vs Inductive Reasoning

Deduction gives you certainty when its premises are true. Induction gives you probability from patterns. Good thinkers know which one they're using, and where each one can fail.

Deductive reasoning: top-down

You start from general premises and reach a conclusion that must be true if the premises are true.

All mammals breathe air. Whales are mammals. Therefore whales breathe air.

An argument is valid if its conclusion follows from its premises, and sound if it is valid and its premises are true. "All birds can fly; penguins are birds; so penguins can fly" is valid but unsound.

Inductive reasoning: bottom-up

You observe specific cases and generalize to a probable conclusion.

Every swan I've seen is white. So probably all swans are white. (Black swans exist in Australia.)

Inductive strength depends on sample size, how representative the sample is, and whether there's a known mechanism behind the pattern.

Abductive reasoning: best explanation

You pick the most likely explanation for what you observe. Doctors diagnosing and detectives solving cases do this. It's powerful but always open to revision.

Practice

"The sun has risen every day in recorded history, so it will rise tomorrow." Deductive or inductive?

Inductive: it generalizes from past observations.

"All squares have four sides. This shape is a square. So it has four sides." Deductive or inductive?

Deductive: the conclusion follows necessarily.
