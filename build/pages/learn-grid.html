How to Solve Logic Grid Puzzles

Logic grids, also called Einstein or zebra puzzles, look intimidating. They're really a bookkeeping exercise, and this method works on every one.

The 6-step method

  1. Build the grid. One block for each pair of categories.
  2. Mark direct facts first. ✓ for yes, ✗ for no.
  3. Fill the row. Once a ✓ goes in, the rest of its row and column in that block become ✗.
  4. Last one standing. If a row has one blank cell left, it must be ✓.
  5. Transfer across blocks. If Ana = Red and Red = Tea, then Ana = Tea.
  6. Work the comparative clues. "Older than", "left of" and "not next to" rule out positions at the ends.

Worked example

Three friends (Ana, Ben, Cy) each own one pet (cat, dog, fish) and drive one car color (red, blue, green).
1. Ana doesn't own the dog.
2. The fish owner drives the green car.
3. Ben drives the red car.
4. Cy doesn't own the cat.
5. Ana doesn't drive the green car.

Step 1 — transfer: Ben drives red (clue 3), and the fish owner drives green (clue 2), so Ben ✗ fish.

Step 2 — transfer again: Ana ✗ green (clue 5), so Ana ✗ fish.

Step 3 — last one standing: neither Ana nor Ben owns the fish, so Cy owns the fish and drives green.

Step 4: Ana ✗ dog (clue 1) and the fish is taken, so Ana owns the cat, and Ben owns the dog.

Step 5: Ben is red and Cy is green, so Ana drives blue. Solved, with a unique answer.

Pro tips

  • Use pencil marks for "maybe" states on hard grids.
  • Re-read each clue after every new ✓. Clues often come back into play.
  • For "either/or" clues, split into cases and look for a contradiction.
  • Speed-solvers read every clue once before marking anything.

Put it into practice with Daily Sudoku (the same elimination logic) and the Monthly Championship.
